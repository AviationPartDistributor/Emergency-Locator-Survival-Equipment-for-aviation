## Understanding Different Types of Aircraft ELTs

Not every emergency locator transmitter performs the same function. The installation and activation characteristics vary according to the type of ELT.

### Automatic Fixed ELT

An automatic fixed ELT is permanently attached to the aircraft and is intended to remain with the aircraft following an emergency event. Its installation and antenna arrangement are important because the system must be positioned to provide the intended emergency transmission capability.

### Automatic Portable ELT

An automatic portable ELT can be attached to the aircraft during flight and removed after an emergency. Depending on the equipment design, it may be used to support the location of survivors after they leave the aircraft.

### Automatic Deployable ELT

An automatic deployable ELT is designed to deploy and activate automatically under specified emergency conditions. Certain configurations are designed to remain operational in water and assist search-and-rescue teams in locating the aircraft.

### Survival ELT

A survival ELT is removable from the aircraft and intended for use by survivors. It may be manually activated and, depending on the design and applicable requirements, may incorporate automatic or water-activated functions.

### Distress Tracking ELT

Distress tracking equipment is designed to provide emergency information when specified distress conditions are detected. These systems represent another development in aircraft emergency location technology.

Understanding these differences helps aircraft procurement teams identify the correct equipment rather than sourcing an ELT based only on a generic description.

## ELT Antennas and Associated Components

The ELT itself is only one part of an emergency locator installation. Antennas, cables, connectors, switches, mounting hardware, and other components can all affect the overall installation.

An ELT antenna must be compatible with the associated transmitter and aircraft installation. Factors can include:

- Antenna type
- Connector configuration
- Frequency requirements
- Mounting arrangement
- Cable configuration
- Electrical characteristics
- Aircraft installation location
- Equipment model

For this reason, maintenance teams should avoid selecting an antenna based solely on physical appearance.

When replacing an antenna, providing the ELT manufacturer, model, aircraft model, and existing part number can make the sourcing process more precise.

## ELT Batteries and Power Requirements

Battery condition is an important consideration for emergency locator equipment because ELTs depend on their power source to transmit an emergency signal.

Battery-related procurement requirements may include:

- Battery part number
- ELT model
- Manufacturer
- Battery type
- Expiration date
- Useful life
- Recharge requirements
- Installation configuration
- Environmental requirements
- Applicable maintenance documentation

Aviation maintenance teams should monitor battery service life and replacement requirements as part of their regular emergency equipment maintenance program.

A replacement battery should not be selected simply because it has the same physical dimensions or nominal electrical characteristics. Compatibility with the specific ELT model and applicable maintenance requirements should be confirmed.

## Personal Locator Beacons and Portable Emergency Location

Personal Locator Beacons, commonly known as PLBs, provide portable emergency location capability for an individual.

Unlike an aircraft-mounted ELT, a PLB is intended to remain with the person carrying it. This can become particularly useful when survivors move away from the aircraft or when the aircraft's primary emergency locator equipment is no longer positioned with the survivors.

When sourcing a PLB, aviation procurement teams should verify:

- Manufacturer
- Model
- Part number
- Approval or certification requirements
- Battery condition
- Registration requirements
- Operating characteristics
- Environmental suitability

PLBs can complement fixed and portable aircraft emergency locator equipment when they are appropriate for the operation.
